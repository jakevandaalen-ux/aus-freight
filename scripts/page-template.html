      Today's website charges come from the October 2025 shipping sheet and new charges from the proposed rates sheets (RRG Website Shipping AU, proposed rates, October 2026, break-even settings; the version with a Bulky size while the Bulky switch is on, where any item of 0.4 m³ or more, i.e. cubic weight 100 kg+, makes the order Bulky) for the order's size (the website's 1–5 score: the highest of longest side, cubic weight at 250 kg/m³ and actual weight, taken from the order's biggest item) and include GST. Australia Post is priced from your contract rates (pricing schedule dated 18 May 2026, GST inclusive): the greater of dead weight or cubic weight (250 kg/m³) per parcel, up to 22 kg, on the cheapest of the Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and Perth accounts the warehouse uses (Brendale, Campbellfield, Adelaide and Wangara each use their own; Moorebank, Moonah and Fyshwick use all four), plus an 11.5% fuel surcharge (October 2026, worked out from your shipments; it changes monthly). Checked against 3,597 past Australia Post shipments, it matches the carrier's estimate within 3% on 98.6% of them. The other carriers (StarTrack Road Express and Premium, TNT, Aramex, Northline, Cochranes, Followmont, PEP, Morrows and the Civic couriers) are priced from the OpenFreight rate cards and the zone lists exported on 23 October 2026, for the warehouse's own origin zone. Their fuel levies and surcharges aren't on the cards, so each card price is multiplied by that service's typical ratio of invoiced cost (or estimate where not yet billed) to card price for August–October 2026 shipments with nothing over 1.2 m, and a typical surcharge is added for orders with an item over 1.2 m, 1.8 m or 2.4 m, measured the same way. Hi-Trans, Civic NSW and Jet Couriers are left out because we haven't shipped with them, so there's nothing to calibrate against. The line under the carriers compares with past orders: the median we were actually charged (invoiced, or the estimate where not yet billed, plus GST) on past matched orders of the same website size, sent as one shipment from the same warehouse to the same state and Australia Post zone, with the longest item on the same side of 1.05 m and a similar chargeable weight (the greater of weight or cubic × 250). If there aren't at least six, it widens the weight range, then to any destination in that zone, then to any warehouse. Australia Post is ruled out for items over 1.05 m long, 22 kg or 0.25 m³. With "Follow the freight guide" ticked, every carrier also follows the RRG freight/dispatch guide: StarTrack Road Express up to 20 kg, 1.17 m × 0.6 m × 0.6 m per item; StarTrack Premium up to 22 kg, 1.05 m × 0.6 m × 0.6 m; Aramex up to 25 kg, 2.0 m and 1.05 m³, same-state only; TNT up to 70 kg and 2.4 × 1.2 × 1.5 m, and only when no other approved carrier can take the order; Morrows (VIC), Cochranes (SA) up to 30 kg and 3.0 m; Followmont (QLD) up to 6.0 m; PEP (WA) up to 25 kg, 1.2 × 0.6 × 0.6 m and 0.49 m³, each only within its own state. Northline and the Civic couriers aren't in the guide, so they're shown as needing approval. Capital Transport is no longer used, so it's left out. Product sizes, prices and costs come from your product export (20 October 2026).

      Australia Post zones come from Australia Post's MyPost Business postcode list: Metro = Capital City (the eight capitals), Outer metro = Major Areas (e.g. Gold Coast, Newcastle, Geelong, Launceston), Regional = Rural (each state's "Outer" postcodes), plus Remote Islands & Locations. Australia Post notes that TAS Other postcodes don't get Zone 1 pricing. Postcode locations and remoteness (ABS Remoteness Areas 2021) come from the Australian Postcodes dataset; each dot sits at the average position of the postcode's suburbs, PO-box and business-only postcodes (rings) are placed at their post office. Remote islands and the Antarctic postcode are drawn in dashed boxes at the map's edge, out of their true position. Australia Post's ranges also include about 800 numbers that aren't real postcodes; those can't be shown. State outlines are simplified from Natural Earth via world-geojson. The ACT sits inside the NSW outline. Warehouses are placed at their suburb's centre, and "postcodes" beside each one counts the postcodes it is closest to in a straight line (road distance will differ).
